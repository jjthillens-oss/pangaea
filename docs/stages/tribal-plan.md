# Tribal stage plan — PROPOSED

Status: **PROPOSED**. Design authority: [GENESIS Bible](../bible/GENESIS_Master_Developer_Bible_v0.1.pdf) §§4, 9, 12–14, 18–19. The [playthrough research](../research/spore/PLAYTHROUGH_VIDEO_NOTES.md) records a village, food gathering, tools, a neighboring tribe and peaceful or hostile interaction. These are loop observations only; no Spore code, art, interface or fixed presentation is reused. This stage follows Social and precedes Civilization; the [earlier proposal](../proposals/tribal-era.md) supplies candidates, not approvals.

## Player loop and first settlement

**Observe the territory → gather → assign work → build → meet a neighbor → respond to shortages → adapt.** The player begins with a recognizable Social-era group at a small campsite, not a new anonymous faction. Body, diet, trust and relationship history continue to affect tasks. Food and material sites have regeneration and ownership constraints. Time spent gathering or negotiating must create a visible change in the settlement and its neighbors. The first playable slice has one campsite, two resource sites, a functional shelter, a small group with distinct roles, and one rival tribe.

The player can directly control their creature to explore, feed, observe and negotiate. A wider view lets them assign bounded intentions and inspect work queues. Moving between views must preserve orders and identity, and embodied play must remain useful. A return to a body or tool editor after a meaningful settlement milestone previews tradeoffs rather than granting free parts; exact triggers and economy require owner approval.

## Work, tools and construction

- Assign gather, carry, guard, construct and rest intentions to individuals. They acknowledge tasks and explain blocked routes or unavailable anatomy. Tasks time out or recover; they cannot keep a worker stuck forever.
- A shared reservation owns each harvestable item or material delivery. If two workers choose the same item, one gets another target or waits visibly. Depletion and regeneration are bounded; a canceled or failed job releases reservations.
- Tools extend anatomy. A grasping body may carry an object directly; a body without hands needs a viable, explicitly designed alternate handling route such as a fitted carrier or team assistance. Cost and speed differ, but progression is not denied by body plan.
- Construction is a functional module with placement preview, collision and access validation, stated materials, and an explicit refund policy. Preview and commit consult the same rules. A canceled build cannot create materials. Queue inspection and pause expose reservations and remaining work.

| First-slice element | Purpose | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Food site and material site | Force an allocation choice | Gatherers do not duplicate a pickup; supply can recover |
| Shelter module | Protect rest and store a bounded reserve | Placement remains reachable; shortage has a remedy |
| Rival tribe | Test trade, cooperation and territorial conflict | Both groups retain identity and consequences after reload |

## Neighbor and conflict

The rival tribe has needs, a territory and a record of encounters. Approach can lead to exchange, assistance, warning, retreat or conflict, with exact offers and consequences shown before commitment. The same interface must not label every dispute as permanent evil. Repeated actions may shape culture, but one collision cannot lock the entire future. Combat uses readable intent and disengagement rules; peaceful play remains a supported way to secure food or access. A shortage can be solved by adjusting jobs, choosing a new site or making a bounded exchange, rather than by compulsory raiding.

## Continuity and exit

The founding creature remains playable. Social companions keep identities, roles and meaningful relationships; ancestral traits affect diet, movement, communication and handling. Earlier aquatic habitats and land routes remain part of the world. Saving includes settlement resources, reservations, construction state and neighbor history only after a separate schema and migration review. A recoverable checkpoint precedes any Civilization transition; it should arise from functioning connected settlements and infrastructure, not simply a filled progress bar. No such transition is approved by this plan.

## Acceptance evidence

1. Establish a functioning shelter, resolve a real resource shortage, and interact with the rival peacefully or through bounded conflict.
2. Issue work to multiple individuals; duplicate resource claims and cancellation create no food or material.
3. A body lacking hands has a viable, measurable handling path; inherited anatomy still changes the outcome.
4. Save/reload preserves named group members, neighbor relationship, stocks and active work without duplication or deadlock.
5. Direct creature play remains useful after the strategic view opens. Rendered accessibility, input, audio and visual checks are separate from headless tests.
