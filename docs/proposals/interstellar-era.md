# Interstellar era - PROPOSED

Status: **PROPOSED** throughout. This follows the working seventh era in GENESIS Bible v0.1 sections 4 and 11. The name, boundary, and scope still require owner approval. No milestone or save-schema change is proposed.

## Core loop - PROPOSED

Prepare a bounded expedition, compare destinations with species needs, travel with a recoverable plan, survey one new habitat, establish a viable outpost, exchange knowledge or resources, and return home. The homeworld remains a place of consequential play rather than a launch-menu backdrop. A small authored star region with seeded destination conditions tests this loop; infinite content is outside the milestone.

**Smallest playable first slice - PROPOSED:** homeworld plus one reachable second world, one ship configuration with a visible range/capacity/habitat budget, one survey site, one outpost requirement, and a complete return trip. The slice tests interrupted travel and an unavailable destination without losing the pre-departure checkpoint.

## Anatomy, earlier choices, and editors - PROPOSED

Inherited diet, breathing or environmental tolerance, locomotion, and social preferences determine what an outpost must supply; none changes silently at launch. A civilization-era trade route and diplomatic relationship can affect expedition supplies or contact. Local embodied play on home and destination worlds remains useful for surveying and observing actual ecological consequences.

A bounded ship editor chooses from approved modules and previews range, capacity, habitat support, and recoverability before departure. An outpost placement preview compares local conditions against biological needs. The creature editor still applies to a lineage through its established validation and cost rules; ship modules do not replace anatomy or ancestry. Arbitrary ship forms and seamless planetary travel require separate technical decisions.

## Threats and relationships - PROPOSED

Unavailable routes, depleted supplies, unsuitable habitats, and ambiguous first contact require early warnings and recovery paths. Contact uses readable goals and remembered encounters, not a single irreversible surprise choice. Distant ecosystems use bounded simulation; revisiting a site must preserve discoveries and named relationships. A return to the homeworld can reveal a changed trade commitment or ecological pressure inherited from civilization play.

## Carryover and transition - PROPOSED

The player chooses departure only after the expedition is viable, with a recoverable checkpoint before travel. The prior civilization-era shipment or local-settlement activity remains directly playable on returning home. A founder's dietary or swimming adaptation still affects at least one habitat-support or survey choice. A named homeworld ally or rival remains present in the expedition's trade or contact history. The same lineage, culture, discoveries, world records, and relationships persist across outbound travel and return; implementation needs separately approved persistence transactions and failure tests.

## Departure from the Bible - PROPOSED

Bible section 11 asks for a small authored region, seeded destinations, viable outpost, and return. Limiting the first slice to a two-world round trip makes those acceptance points testable before travel presentation or planetary scale is decided. The concrete ship and outpost previews are proposed ways to expose the Bible's functional constraints, not approval of a ship asset pipeline or travel technology.

## Open questions for the owner - PROPOSED

1. Which homeworld activity should pull the player back after the first outpost is established?
2. Which biological need best differentiates the first destination from home without forcing a new body?
3. What recovery choice should be offered for a failed outbound leg or an unavailable destination?
