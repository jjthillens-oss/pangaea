# Social era - PROPOSED

Status: **PROPOSED** throughout. This follows the working fourth era in GENESIS Bible v0.1 sections 4 and 8. The name, boundary, and scope still require owner approval. No milestone or save-schema change is proposed.

## Core loop - PROPOSED

Meet an individual, read a signal, offer help or propose a shared task, give a bounded order, and observe the response and later relationship. Feeding and survival remain available solo strategies. Group success should make one route safer or more productive without making companionship mandatory.

**Smallest playable first slice - PROPOSED:** one familiar shoreline habitat, one named potential companion, one food-gathering task, and a safe separation/reunion route. The player can recruit, order follow/wait/assist, see an explicit refusal or retreat, dismiss, and reload without losing identity or a significant encounter. Unreachable orders time out with an explanation.

## Anatomy, earlier choices, and editor - PROPOSED

Inherited locomotion and diet still determine which route and food the player can use. Sensory and signaling organs may change communication range or channel at a cost in energy or body budget. An accessible visual/text substitute is always provided; a body lacking one signal organ is not locked out of the basic command set. Earlier nest locations can serve as meeting points.

The creature editor adds a comparison of communication reach and cost alongside its existing movement, feeding, and stability preview. A separate, small group-role panel assigns a willing companion a bounded role; it does not rewrite that individual's genome. Any inheritable edit retains the existing validate-preview-commit transaction.

## Threats and relationships - PROPOSED

Predators and food shortages create reasons to coordinate. Companions can retreat, be separated, or decline a dangerous order; all three outcomes need visible acknowledgment. Recent encounters affect trust, while a persistent reputation records repeated behavior. One accidental bump cannot permanently convert a neighbor into an enemy. Group tasks use observable intent and bounded recovery rather than omniscient knowledge.

## Carryover and transition - PROPOSED

The player chooses the transition only after a survivable group activity is demonstrated. A pre-transition checkpoint and recoverable previous save protect the lineage. The earlier terrestrial foraging and swimming routes remain directly playable. A swimming adaptation still helps a companion task across water. A named shoreline rival or ally retains its relationship and encounter history. Identity, ancestry, ecology, and discoveries continue; any persistence implementation needs separate approval and migration tests.

## Departure from the Bible - PROPOSED

Bible section 8 specifies roles, orders, relationships, and group-task acceptance but not a first map. Reusing one shoreline route keeps the slice small and tests whether social decisions change a known survival problem. The group-role panel is a proposed presentation for those rules, not a new progression resource.
