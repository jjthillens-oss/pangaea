# Tribal era - PROPOSED

Status: **PROPOSED** throughout. This follows the working fifth era in GENESIS Bible v0.1 sections 4 and 9. The name, boundary, and scope still require owner approval. No milestone or save-schema change is proposed.

## Core loop - PROPOSED

Explore a local territory, gather a needed resource, assign a small group to a task, place one useful structure, resolve a shortage, and speak with a neighbor. Pausing to inspect jobs should expose reservations and expected costs. Embodied feeding, observation, and travel remain meaningful ways to learn whether plans work.

**Smallest playable first slice - PROPOSED:** one established group, a single campsite, two resource sites, one shelter module, and one neighboring group. The player can complete construction, see a contested resource reservation, cancel with an explained refund, and resolve one shortage by changing work or making an explicit exchange.

## Anatomy, earlier choices, and editors - PROPOSED

Inherited diet changes food demand; mobility changes gathering routes; grasping or carrying anatomy changes how tools and materials can be handled. Approved alternatives provide viable construction paths for bodies without hand-like anatomy. A familiar companion's role and trust affect who will accept risky work. Water travel remains useful where the campsite touches the prior habitat.

The creature editor continues to preview body tradeoffs. A bounded tool interface shows which attachment or helper enables a task, with comparable time and resource cost. A settlement placement preview checks access, collision, reservation, and affordability before committing. Cancellation and undo follow an explicit policy; no hidden resource duplication is permitted.

## Threats and relationships - PROPOSED

Food shortfall, unsafe paths, and territorial disagreement threaten the campsite. Workers acknowledge impossible orders and recover from blocked routes. The neighboring group's memory distinguishes trade, cooperation, and repeated trespass; it is not reduced to a permanent morality meter. A known terrestrial or social-era individual remains identifiable in the group.

## Carryover and transition - PROPOSED

The transition requires a willing, viable group and a recoverable checkpoint. The social-era companion task remains playable at the campsite. A communication adaptation still changes coordination or negotiation reach. The first recruited companion's relationship persists as a named worker or neighbor. Lineage, local habitat, rival history, and discoveries remain accessible. Persisting structures and reservations needs separate owner approval, transaction design, and migration tests.

## Departure from the Bible - PROPOSED

Bible section 9 calls for a functioning settlement, shortage, and neighbor encounter but does not set its minimum size. One campsite and shelter module form a testable slice before broader construction. Treating reservations as visible work claims makes the Bible's shared-reservation rule understandable to players.
