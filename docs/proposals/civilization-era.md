# Civilization era - PROPOSED

Status: **PROPOSED** throughout. This follows the working sixth era in GENESIS Bible v0.1 sections 4 and 10. The name, boundary, and scope still require owner approval. No milestone or save-schema change is proposed.

## Core loop - PROPOSED

Inspect a bounded region, identify a need in one settlement, route an actual delivery from another, respond to a disrupted route, and see the effect on people and habitat. Research, diplomacy, transport, and production each have prerequisites and costs. A regional view issues intentions; visiting a settlement or creature shows their local consequences.

**Smallest playable first slice - PROPOSED:** two existing settlements, one resource route, one shipment with trackable state, one possible interruption, and one limited agreement with a neighboring group. The player can restore or reroute delivery, observe a changed settlement need, and return to direct creature play. No planet-wide simulation is implied.

## Anatomy, earlier choices, and editors - PROPOSED

Founders' diet and environmental tolerances determine settlement food and habitat needs. Locomotion and carrying anatomy affect local route access and delivery methods; inherited communication capability can influence, but never solely gate, a negotiation. A tribal ally or rival has a remembered position in the region. Energy for an individual, adaptation potential for inherited traits, and knowledge for research remain distinct resources.

The creature editor keeps valid embodied changes available at supported checkpoints. A bounded route editor previews travel time, carrying capacity, exposure, ecological cost, and actual delivery state. A research panel shows concrete prerequisites and practical inputs. A diplomacy preview states the exact offer and commitment before acceptance. These are scoped proposals for distinct interfaces, not a speculative general-purpose editor framework.

## Threats and relationships - PROPOSED

Route disruption, shortage, habitat loss, and territorial disagreement produce readable consequences. Economic, diplomatic, and military options each carry costs, and conflict does not erase a neighbor's history. Regional ecology may summarize distant populations, but a return to a local site preserves named individuals and does not duplicate or silently remove resources at the fidelity boundary.

## Carryover and transition - PROPOSED

The player chooses the transition after demonstrating two viable settlements and receives a pre-transition checkpoint. A tribal-era gather/build task remains directly playable in the founding settlement. An inherited carrying or swimming adaptation still affects a local route. The first neighboring group's agreement or dispute persists and influences a regional decision. Settlement histories, biological needs, lineage, and ecology carry forward. Any regional-state serialization requires a separate approved schema and migration tests.

## Departure from the Bible - PROPOSED

Bible section 10 requires a bounded region and two-settlement exchange but does not prescribe a first transport interface. Following one shipment end to end makes route state inspectable and prevents the appearance of silent teleportation. The single interruption and agreement are a narrow demonstration, not a promise of a full economy or diplomacy campaign.
