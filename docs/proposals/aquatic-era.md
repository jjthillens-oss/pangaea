# Aquatic era — PROPOSED

Status: **PROPOSED** throughout. This is a design proposal, not an implemented feature or an approved change to milestone scope. It develops GENESIS Bible v0.1 §§4, 6, 12, 18–19 and the existing decisions log. The working name and its place in the seven-era outline remain subject to owner review.

## Core loop — PROPOSED

The player observes a small set of connected depth zones, chooses a feeding or shelter route, swims it, encounters competing species, then returns to a spawning site to compare and edit offspring anatomy. A known route changes when prey shifts, a rival occupies a shelter, or a current makes the old body costly to control. Food, energy and reproduction remain legible consequences of play. Discovery records identify the places and species involved, rather than granting generic progress for distance traveled.

The first slice should contain a shallow refuge, one deeper feeding area and one route between them. Depth is introduced through a clear near/far cue and a safe return path. Pressure or oxygen meters are deferred unless a prototype shows choices that players can understand; the Bible explicitly makes them conditional. Population simulation may use detailed local actors and aggregate distant counts, but must preserve identities and avoid duplicate resources at handoff.

## Anatomy and editor — PROPOSED

Body length and mass affect acceleration and turning; fins and tails trade burst speed for energy use and low-speed control; sensory organs trade reach or directional information for body cost. A buoyancy adaptation can lower the effort of holding depth but reduce quick descent. The editor previews those comparisons on the same short route, including feeding reach and energy demand, before spending adaptation potential. It validates support and collision at extreme proportions. Procedurally generated body skin follows the owner-approved creature-editor direction in `docs/DECISIONS.md`; detail parts still require Blender-authored sources and records.

Traits inherited from the microscopic genome retain stable identity. A cell-stage mouth or feeding preference maps to an aquatic dietary capability with a visible explanation, rather than silently becoming a new part. This mapping is an owner decision and needs a tested transition contract before implementation. No save format change is proposed here.

## Threats and relationships — PROPOSED

Predators telegraph investigation, pursuit and disengagement through pose and movement. Large organisms may first be readable environmental hazards, as the Bible permits, without claiming full simulation. Feeding grounds and shelters form recurring encounters, so a rival's behavior is learned over visits. A lineage that repeatedly competes for one site remains recognizable in the journal and after reload. Accessible shape, motion and labels accompany color and sound cues.

## Carryover and transition — PROPOSED

Entering this era would be a player choice after demonstrated survival capability and a survivable destination, with a pre-transition checkpoint and recoverable previous save. The same lineage ID, genome ancestry, diet, world seed, discoveries and species relationships continue. One microscopic pool remains a playable local activity, and its food or predator relationship remains visible in the larger aquatic ecosystem. A microscopic adaptation still matters through the explained diet mapping. Returning to that earlier habitat is meaningful play, not just a history badge.

No transition, cross-era save transaction, or new schema is authorized by this document. Those require separate scope, migration tests and owner approval. This proposal does not move the M1 first playable or the current seven-era outline.

## Departure from the Bible — PROPOSED

Bible §6 names habitat zones and gradual depth but not a minimum slice. The three-zone route above is a deliberately small test shape for learning whether depth and anatomy change player decisions; it is not a content commitment. Bible §6 names nests or spawning sites as recognizable places; using the same site as the editor return point is a proposed way to connect navigation and reproduction. Neither change alters the transition contract.

3. What evidence would justify adding oxygen or pressure beyond route and predator hazards?
4. How much earlier microscopic habitat should remain directly visitable once aquatic play begins?
