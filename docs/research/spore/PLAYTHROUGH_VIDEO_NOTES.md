# Spore full playthrough: observation notes

Status: PROPOSED research. Observational only; no frames, art or audio from the video are stored in the repo.

Source: a 4 h 31 min (16,239 s) 640x360 playthrough video Joshua shared on 2026-10-24. Method: one frame every
60 s (271 frames, read as contact sheets) plus a few full frames at key moments. At this sampling rate anything
that lasts under a minute may be missed, so the timings below are approximate (±1 min).

## Timeline

| Stage | Video time | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intro (meteor carries life to the planet) | 0:00 | — | cinematic |
| Cell | 0:01 – ~0:09 | ~3% | about 9 minutes |

Takeaway: Spore's early stages are short. Cell is single-digit minutes, creature well under an hour. Pangaea's
cell stage is already longer than Spore's, which fits Joshua's "faster progression" feedback.

## Cell (0:01 – 0:09)

- Top-down 2D-feeling camera over a layered, parallax 3D backdrop: big blurry organisms drift in the deep layers
  and give scale. Deep blue/violet palette (matches Pangaea's approved deep-blue look).
- Growth by zoom: by 0:04 a huge creature fills the frame behind the player; at 0:07 the backdrop changes to
  bright teal and a green plant; at 0:08 a sandy floor with shells. Each size step swaps the whole backdrop and
  what is "food" vs "threat".
- HUD: a single DNA progress bar across the bottom with milestone ticks, a small mission card top left
  ("Collect: unlock cell parts", with a count), and a mini icon bottom right. Almost nothing else on screen.
- Editor (0:03): a dark left panel of part icons in a grid (filter mouths, jaws, spikes, flagella, cilia, eyes),
  the cell centred on a round petri-dish stage to the right. Parts are found by eating debris of other cells
  ("Unlock cell parts"), then bought with DNA.
- Mating call opens the editor; the stage ends with the walk onto land (0:09 to 0:10).

## Creature (0:10 – 0:50)

- Third-person camera behind and above the creature; terrain with alien trees, grass, rain/dark weather passes
  (0:21, 0:35 to 0:44 are night/storm, almost monochrome).
- Nest: the home base is a ring of rocks and sticks with an egg (0:12, 0:16 egg close-up, 0:25 the creature
  sitting on the nest). Other species have their own nests the player walks to.
- Social stance (full frame at 0:12): before any interaction a tutorial explains the stance: green button to
  make friends, red to "make dinner", then ability buttons. The bottom bar holds the ability buttons, health and
  hunger bars; minimap bottom left; a goal list top left ("Impress 3 ... to ally", "Collect: find new parts").
- Social mini-game: sing, dance, charm, pose in front of another species to ally (0:14 a pink pack, 0:31 an ally
  following the player). Allies join the pack and follow (0:29 two creatures, 0:32 a pack of three).
- Combat: bite/charge/strike abilities, prey drops meat. Hunts in packs (0:37 to 0:44).
- Parts come from skeletons/bones found in the world (glowing pickups, 0:18, 0:30 green crosses).
- Mating: the player returns to the nest and calls a mate; a short animation, then the creature editor opens
  (0:25, 0:34, 0:45, 0:49 — four editor visits in about 40 minutes, one every ~8 to 10 minutes).
- Editor: left panel is a grid of part icons by category (mouths, limbs, feet, hands, eyes, details); the
  creature stands on a round stone plinth under trees and sky. Ability levels show as small yellow sun icons
  per part. The creature visibly grows each visit (spine longer, more limbs, bigger head).
- Brain-size meter fills; when full the game moves to tribal.

## Tribal (0:51 – 1:31)

- RTS camera, top-down at an angle. The village is a stone ring around a hut and a fire pit (0:52).
- The player commands several tribe members: gather food from bushes, fish, hunt. Food is the single currency.
- Buildings slot into fixed sockets around the ring (0:58, 1:03: a left build panel with a few icons).
- Rival tribes have their own coloured villages. Two ways to win them: musical instruments (social) or weapons
  (combat). Cut scenes with a totem pole (1:02) and gift/attack banners (1:11, 1:23).
- Outfit editor (1:16, 1:24): the same plinth, now on a wooden stage, adding masks, armour and decorations.
- The village grows to a full ring of buildings; ends with a "city" cinematic (1:31).

## Civilization (1:32 – 2:21)

- Begins with building and vehicle editors (1:33 a land vehicle with the creature's eyes, 1:35, 1:40 building
  placement with glowing link lines between buildings that give bonuses).
- Planet map: own city, rivals, spice geysers (resource). Yellow borders and road lines (1:38, 1:41).
- Capture cities by military, religious or economic vehicles (2:08 to 2:19: arcs of missiles/beams converge on a
  city; red borders are enemies).
- Ends by unifying the planet and launching a spaceship (2:21 cinematic).

## Space (2:22 – 4:31)

- Galaxy map with zoomable star systems (2:23 planets around a sun). Contact dialogs with other empires use a
  portrait, a text box and two-to-three answer buttons (2:22, 3:10, 4:10, 4:14).
- Spaceship flies over planets: terraform, scan, abduct, fight (3:04 to 3:11). Colonies are built with a
  hub-and-slot city editor (3:00, 4:08, 4:12).
- Empire map: coloured territory bubbles linked by trade lines (4:05).
- Badges/medals for achievements (4:02).
- About half the video is space; it is long, repetitive (fly, chat, fight, colonise) and has no end state in view.

## Cross-stage patterns worth adopting

1. **One currency per stage and one progress bar.** DNA, then food, then money (sporebucks), always shown in the
   same bottom bar.
2. **The editor is the reward.** Every stage has an editor visit every few minutes, always the same layout: icons
   left, subject on a round plinth right, test/play button. Pangaea's cell and creature editors already follow
   this; keep one shell for all eras.
3. **Short stages, visible scale jump.** Each stage change swaps camera, controls and goal.
4. **Social vs combat on every stage.** The player picks friend or foe by a stance switch before interacting.
5. **Persistent creature.** The creature built in stage 2 shows up as the tribe, citizen and ship captain.
6. **Minimal HUD.** Goals top left, abilities bottom centre, map bottom left.

## What Pangaea should do better (PROPOSED)

- **Anatomy matters.** In the video, the creature's shape barely changes how it plays; parts are stat stickers.
  Bible §2 (anatomy affects gameplay, every change has a cost) already commits Pangaea to doing better; keep the
  size/speed tradeoffs Joshua asked for.
- **Late stages are thinner.** Tribal and civ are simple RTS loops and space is half the run time but repetitive.
  Pangaea's later eras need fewer, deeper systems rather than more screen time.
- **Night/storm segments look washed out** (0:35 to 0:44 are nearly grey). Pangaea's lighting should keep the
  creature readable at night.
- **Transitions lose the player's work.** Abilities earned per stage are a single pick ("card"); Pangaea keeps a
  genome and lineage through every era (Bible §18 save fields), so the history can carry more weight.

## Conflicts with the GENESIS Bible

- **Era count.** Spore has five stages (cell, creature, tribal, civ, space). The Bible has seven (Microscopic,
  Aquatic, Terrestrial, Social, Tribal, Civilization, Interstellar). The Bible wins: Aquatic and Social are the
  two "added" stages Joshua mentioned. Spore's cell stage maps to Microscopic, creature to Terrestrial (with its
  social mini-game feeding the Social era), tribal/civ/space map one to one.
- **Stage length.** Spore's cell stage is ~9 minutes; the Bible does not fix stage length, but the endless cell
  stage (Joshua's choice) is longer by design. Keep it endless, but pace the editor visits to every few minutes
  as in the video.
- **No copying.** Layout ideas (left icon panel, plinth) are generic editor patterns; Pangaea's art, icons,
  names and UI styling stay original.
