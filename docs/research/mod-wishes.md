# What Spore players built for themselves: a mod wish list

**Status: PROPOSED research, not approved design.** Nothing here changes the Bible, the era count, the milestone
scope or `docs/DECISIONS.md`. It records what players changed in Spore through mods and why, as input for owner
review. Spore features are named only to explain a request; GENESIS reuses no Spore part, name, layout or system.

- **Date:** 2026-10-25 (all sources accessed on this date)
- **Author:** research thread, Pangaea
- **Companion briefs:** `community-wants.md` (fan themes 1 to 17), `innovation-backlog.md` (IB items),
  `spore/LEGAL_RESEARCH_BOUNDARIES.md`, and `pcg-talk-lessons.md` (notes from Kate Compton's GDC talk on procedural
  generation; its "ownership" lesson, letting players find, name and claim things, is the design-side twin of wish 11
  and IB-31).

## Method and boundaries

**What was read.** Public mod descriptions, READMEs and listing pages only: the Spore-Community GitHub organisation
page and two project READMEs, the community's "sporemods" index page (about 110 mods across creative, graphical and
campaign categories), ModDB's Spore game page and three mod pages, four Nexus Mods pages, six Steam discussion
threads, two r/Spore pages (via read-only mirrors) and one EA forum thread. 10 web searches.

**What was not done.** No mod was downloaded, installed or run. No source code, binary, package or file-format
documentation was opened, in line with `spore/LEGAL_RESEARCH_BOUNDARIES.md` and the owner's "Spore files are not
used" decision. Nothing is copied: this brief paraphrases what a mod *does for the player*, not how.

**How the wish list is ranked.** A wish gets one count per distinct mod or thread that expresses it. Strength is
high when the wish has either a popularity signal (download or endorsement counts, repeated recommendation in
threads) or several independent mods doing the same thing. The mod scene is small and skews toward creators who make
and share creatures, so editor wishes are over-represented relative to the general player base covered in
`community-wants.md`.

## What the mods say about Spore that reviews do not

1. **The editors were the game for its most committed players.** Roughly half of the indexed mods touch an editor:
   limits, parts, colours, backgrounds, test-drive animations. Players did not want a new editor; they wanted the
   existing one with its ceilings removed. For GENESIS this supports the Bible's "creation integrated with play"
   pillar, and it argues for generous, *explained* limits rather than hard caps: a complexity budget that trades
   against energy or mass (§12) is a limit players can reason about, where Spore's flat cap was not.
2. **Players fixed the space stage before anyone fixed the tribal stage.** Four of the top five wishes are late-game
   fixes: fewer interruptions, less grind, a beatable enemy, shorter travel. Mods that extend the middle stages
   exist but are fewer and later (2020 onward). This matches theme 1 and theme 4 of `community-wants.md` and warns
   that era 6 and 7 pacing is where a seven-era game is most likely to lose players.
3. **Every grind mod is a tuning complaint.** Cargo, spice, trade-route and drive mods change numbers, not systems.
   GENESIS should keep these as tuning data (§12 "numerical curves are tuning data") and expose a difficulty preset
   that moves them, so the equivalent wish is a menu option, not a mod.
4. **Sharing is fragile.** Three independent sources warn that custom parts break sharing with vanilla players, and
   one creator advertises "vanilla compatible" as a feature. Any GENESIS sharing format (IB-09) should carry the
   part list it needs and degrade visibly when a part is missing, rather than refusing to open.
5. **Players want to be a different body in the same world.** "Play as an epic", shrink, grow, pick up smaller
   creatures: these are anatomy-as-gameplay requests (CONFIRMED requirement) that Spore's fixed player scale could
   not satisfy. A supported size range with real consequences for feeding, speed and threat is an original answer.
6. **The scene is tiny and brittle.** Most Steam threads about mods are about installing them, many mods are marked
   broken or cancelled, and the biggest mod has 193K downloads against tens of millions of players. Mod wishes are
   a strong signal about *what the dedicated players valued* and a weak signal about *how many* players want it.

## Scope notes

- Wish 15 (mod tooling) and any request for user scripting fall under the Bible's §2 exclusions and are recorded
  only. Whether GENESIS supports data-file mods at all is an owner decision for a later milestone.
- Wish 2 names third-party parts (dinosaurs, a sister game's parts, licensed robots). GENESIS parts stay original
  (§15). The lesson is variety and category breadth, not those specific parts.
- Nothing here changes the era count or the M1 to M3 scope.
