# Stage transitions and carryover — PROPOSED research

Status: **PROPOSED** throughout. This compares public descriptions and reviews, not game files or implementations. "Duration" describes the player's handover interaction, not a measured loading time; a source that does not publish a duration is marked accordingly. Niche and Species have no discrete cell-to-creature stage: their island and lineage changes are useful contrasts rather than equivalent stages. No text, tables, assets, or code are copied from these games.

## Six comparisons — PROPOSED lessons

### Spore (Cell → Creature)

- **Carry/reset/explanation.** A player-designed species continues through the Cell, Creature, Tribe, Civilization, and Space stages. The creature editor makes the new body visible, while the activity, scale, and goals change sharply. The publisher describes the five distinct stages and the creature-design interface; it does not publish a field-by-field carryover contract. Treat claims about exact resources or map persistence as unknown here. [EA product description](https://www.ea.com/games/spore/spore); [Steam publisher description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/17390/SPORE/).
- **Interaction, duration, return.** The handover uses an editor and a short presentation sequence, not a fixed number of gameplay turns. The store advertises starting at a selected stage, which is a fresh start option rather than proof of returning to an earlier stage within one save. Exact sequence duration is not published in the sources above.
- **Reception.** [Wired's contemporary review](https://www.wired.com/2008/09/spore-review/) praises attachment to a personally made creature and the accessible editors, while criticizing shallow, repetitive stage play. The transition's spectacle cannot compensate for an earlier ability losing its practical role.
- **Pangaea idea — PROPOSED.** Carry a visibly recognizable lineage and one testable cell adaptation into the first aquatic body. **Trap:** treat a camera change and editor reveal as the entire continuity system. Existing observation work is in `docs/research/spore/SPORE_RESEARCH.md`; this section does not reproduce its design details.

### Thrive (Microbe → Multicellular)

- **Carry/reset/explanation.** Its published design moves from an individual microbe to a cell colony and retains editable cell types; in the multicellular editor, a cell type remains editable and changes propagate to its copies. The public design does not specify a Pangaea-like world/save transaction or which environmental objects persist. The developer describes transition toward multicellularity as a player decision. [Microbe progression](https://wiki.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/wiki/Microbe_Progression); [Multicellular stage](https://wiki.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/wiki/Multicellular_Stage).
- **Interaction, duration, return.** Reproduction returns the player to an editor; the stage design calls for editing colony composition rather than a one-time cutscene. No fixed real-time transition duration or guaranteed within-save reversal is specified in these documents. [Multicellular design document](https://wiki.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/wiki/Multicellular_Stage_GDD).
- **Reception.** The developer's [microbe-stage retrospective](https://www.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/devblog-50-microbe-stage-complete) emphasizes the long work needed to make the first stage enjoyable. A [community critique](https://community.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/t/a-thrive-0-5-3-1-in-depth-review-and-critique/3538) finds the early loop initially engaging but later slow and repetitive. These are observations about pacing, not evidence that a planned future stage is already complete.
- **Pangaea idea — PROPOSED.** Show a cell-to-organ conversion card in the editor, including the carried function and cost. **Trap:** defer all progression payoff to an ambitious future stage. Thrive is a design reference only; no GPL material is used.

### Niche (island and generation changes)

- **Carry/reset/explanation.** Niche is a genetic, turn-based survival game, not a sequence of radically different eras. Offspring inherit a combination of genes and a migrating group takes living members to a new island; local food, hazards, and terrain differ. The game communicates this through offspring bodies and the new island's immediate survival pressures. [Developer introduction](https://store.epicgames.com/news/out-now-niche-a-genetics-survival-game?lang=en-US); [Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/440650/Niche_a_genetics_survival_game/); [island review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/54813/niche-a-genetics-survival-game-switch-eshop-review-faq).
- **Interaction, duration, return.** Breeding and migration are turn-based player actions, not a timed cinematic. Travel is a choice at an island exit; the reviewed island progression offers new resources and hazards. The cited sources do not establish a general backtrack rule or fixed minute count.
- **Reception.** The [Nintendo World Report review](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/54813/niche-a-genetics-survival-game-switch-eshop-review-faq) values the changing island challenges but notes that staying with a stable population can become dull. Genetics make a carried trait legible when the next habitat changes its usefulness.
- **Pangaea idea — PROPOSED.** Put a small, readable destination preview before commitment, showing whether inherited feeding and locomotion will work there. **Trap:** allow a migration into an environment where the chosen lineage has no recoverable food or movement path.

### Species: Artificial Life, Real Evolution (continuous lineage)

- **Carry/reset/explanation.** This is an observational evolution sandbox rather than a staged player campaign. Creatures inherit modified genes; the developer advertises a clade diagram, satellite map, and inspection tools that explain lineages while the simulation continues. There is no evidenced discrete stage reset, timed handover, or earlier-era revisit in the published description. [Developer's Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/774541/Species_Artificial_Life_Real_Evolution/).
- **Interaction, duration, return.** The player observes and intervenes continuously; lineage change is seen through simulation and inspection, so there is no cutscene duration to report. The same world remains the object of observation instead of a stage screen to revisit.
- **Reception.** The store page cites praise for close observation of evolution, while a [player review](https://steamcommunity.com/app/774541/discussions/0/3075370124707956701/) criticizes changes that look biologically arbitrary or hard to interpret. This contrast matters more to Pangaea than a nonexistent stage transition.
- **Pangaea idea — PROPOSED.** Show an ancestry trail beside the new body and a concrete reason each retained trait matters. **Trap:** present a new appearance without a readable causal link from old anatomy to current ability.

### Ancestors: The Humankind Odyssey (generation and evolution leaps)

- **Carry/reset/explanation.** The developer describes passing discoveries and physical abilities to future generations while the player expands a clan. It is generational replacement in an explorable territory, rather than an editor that rebuilds a single creature. Individual hominids age out; lineage knowledge is the meaningful carryover. [Developer/publisher description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/536270/Ancestors_The_Humankind_Odyssey/).
- **Interaction, duration, return.** The player chooses to advance generations or evolve after exploration and learning; the resulting time leap changes the playable clan. The official description gives a span of millions of in-world years but no fixed real-world animation duration. A player cannot literally un-age that generation through the progression choice; save reloading is separate.
- **Reception.** An [independent review](https://as.com/meristation/2019/08/25/analisis/1566755068_168746.html) praises discovery and the feeling that actions contribute to human history, but finds sparse guidance overwhelming and some actions repetitive. A transition can feel earned while still needing an explicit explanation of its consequences.
- **Pangaea idea — PROPOSED.** Require demonstrated survival and show a before/after lineage summary before the handover. **Trap:** hide irreversible losses behind a vague evolution button.

### Civilization VI: Rise and Fall (era and Age shift)

- **Carry/reset/explanation.** An empire's cities, map, research, diplomacy, and units continue across eras. Historic Moments earn Era Score; the next Age adds a Normal, Golden, Dark, or Heroic modifier and a dedication choice rather than replacing the map. This is an explicit progress signal, not biological evolution. [Official Civilization VI overview](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vi/); [official Rise and Fall patch notes](https://support.civilization.com/hc/en-us/articles/37687176307731-Patch-Notes-Spring-Update).
- **Interaction, duration, return.** The handover is a turn boundary and choice panel, with no fixed real-time cutscene duration. The old map remains playable but the previous Age's choice cannot simply be reopened as the active Age. An older save would be a separate rollback.
- **Reception.** The [Steam expansion discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/289070/discussions/0/1700542332319996934/) shows players liked the opportunity to recover into a stronger Age but disliked Era Score that felt detached from meaningful play. That is a criticism of opaque scoring rather than continuity of the empire itself.
- **Pangaea idea — PROPOSED.** State the handover's criteria early and let a chosen carryover benefit be legible on the next screen. **Trap:** turn stage readiness into an unexplained score grind.

## Cell → creature handover recommendation — PROPOSED

This proposal interprets “creature” as the first embodied aquatic organism after the Microscopic era, not a new eighth era or a skip to land. It follows the seven-era handoff in `docs/proposals/README.md`, the specific carryover/route design in `docs/proposals/aquatic-era.md`, and the subsequent water-to-land continuity in `docs/proposals/terrestrial-era.md`. It does **not** alter `docs/milestones/M1.md`, `docs/milestones/m1-step3-plan.md`, the current save schema, or the approved scope of the next build.

1. **Eligibility — PROPOSED.** Unlock a transition preview after a lineage survives an observable ecological challenge and reaches a viable spawning place. Show the evidence in play terms—feeding, escape, and reproduction—rather than a hidden stage score. Offer a survivable destination with food and a retreat route. This adopts Niche's habitat consequence and Civilization's progress clarity.
2. **Preview and choice — PROPOSED.** A single comparison panel shows lineage identity, ancestry, diet, and one or two concrete inherited abilities. For each microscopic part or capability, display its proposed aquatic analogue, retained function, cost, and any inability to carry it directly. Let the player preview a short swim-and-feed route before confirming. This borrows the explanatory value of a continuous lineage view without copying any game's editor layout.
3. **Handover transaction — PROPOSED.** Present a clear confirmation and a recoverable pre-transition checkpoint. The first aquatic body retains the same lineage and world seed; one familiar pool and a named rival or ecological relationship remain identifiable. The editor change and camera movement should be brief enough that the first new action—swim, feed, return—arrives promptly. Exact timing is a prototype measurement, not a promise here.
4. **First playable loop — PROPOSED.** Use the shallow refuge, deeper feeding area, and connecting route from `docs/proposals/aquatic-era.md`. A retained microscopic feeding adaptation should visibly affect the route; a fin or tail choice changes movement and energy. The first return to the spawning site demonstrates that the new body belongs to the old lineage. Keep the microscopic habitat meaningfully playable as proposed there, subject to a separately approved transition/save contract.
5. **Failure and revision — PROPOSED.** If the destination fails its food or movement viability checks, block confirmation with a reason and let the player revise the preview body. Avoid spawning a helpless organism and then calling the outcome evolution. Record the handover in a lineage journal, not as a cosmetic trophy.

Open owner questions — **PROPOSED**: Which microscopic abilities must survive as actions on day one? Is the earlier pool directly revisitable or represented as a local activity from the aquatic map? Which relationship—rival, prey, or shelter—best proves ecological continuity? What objective survival evidence unlocks the preview? What checkpoint and migration work would be needed under the separately approved save contract?
